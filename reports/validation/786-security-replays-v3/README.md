## What the three families establish, and what they do not

- Cross-scope write authority (W, 13 cases): a live head is writable only by a writer eligible
  for its scope; refusals are non-disclosing and byte-identical to the unknown-id result; the
  binding survives restart; malformed and superset overrides are refused as documented; a
  complete-scope override commits **because `overrides` is privileged host input** (W4/W4b
  prove host re-declaration, never end-user isolation); refused attempts leave no event,
  identifier or text in any public history payload; a former owner's answers are the same with
  and without a re-creation (W7c and its control).
- Deleted lineage and restart (L, 10 cases): a governed deletion withholds its generation from
  every read; re-creations are isolated both ways (L3 and its control); deleted versions are
  as-of barriers; partial deletion withholds only that version, live and after restart; a
  deleted head or lineage member cannot be deleted again and the recorded tombstone (exact
  `deleted_at`, version, lineage) is unchanged by the attempt; after a permanent deletion the
  canonical substrate holds no fact of the lineage, no public read serves it, and the derived
  reconciliation reports complete.
- Public tombstone and observer separation (R, 8 cases): the owner reads only its tombstone,
  live and after restart, with no rejected values; another project reads the never-existed
  shape field for field; each deleting scope reads the version its own deletion recorded (A v3,
  B v2); derivations of deleted sources are withheld while live derivations keep provenance;
  refused and parked proposals carry no former count.

Not established: process-level restart (every `reopen` is a handle close and open in one
process); erasure beyond the canonical substrate and public reads (journals, WAL frames before
checkpoint, backups, snapshots: docs/28, docs/44); the inertness of text tokens in history
payloads is a property of the audit event schema, so the payload scans prove identifier
absence and the absence of text that the schema could not carry anyway (pre-execution review
F-6). Positive controls (`controls`) and implementation evidence (`record`) are labelled in
every fixture; the latter never count toward acceptance.
